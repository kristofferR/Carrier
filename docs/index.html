A quick backstory

Why Carrier exists

Wasn't there a Messenger desktop app once?

Meta discontinued it a while back.

Shame - I liked having my chats in their own window.

Try Carrier. It's a desktop app that shows Messenger in its own window ✈️

What's inside

Everything Carrier adds to Messenger

Only Messenger

Facebook's Feed, Reels and Marketplace are hidden. Carrier shows only your conversations.

Reply from notifications

Reply, react or mark as read without opening Carrier. Notifications show the sender's photo.

Unread count on the Dock or taskbar

Show the number or just a dot, in the Dock, taskbar, menu bar or tray.

Hide that you're typing

The people you chat with don't see “typing…” while you write.

Right-click menus

React to, reply to or forward a message. Mute, archive or mark a conversation as read.

Themes and app icons

Nine themes, an accent color, a message font and four app icons.

Schedule messages

Write a message now and send it at a date and time you choose.

Export conversations

Save a conversation as an HTML or JSON file, straight from this computer.

About 4 MB

Carrier uses your computer's own web engine instead of bundling a browser.

Notifications

Reply, react or mark as read from the notification.

Notifications show the sender's photo, or the group's picture. Pause them for an hour or until tomorrow morning, and Messenger's own Do Not Disturb, set on your phone or in Carrier, silences them too.

Scheduled messages

Write it now, send it later.

Type a message, click the clock next to the emoji button and pick a time: in 15 minutes, in an hour, this evening, tomorrow morning, or any date and time you choose. Carrier sends it for you while it's running and online. You can change or cancel it until then.

Appearance

Themes, accent colors and app icons.

Pick from nine themes, from Graphite and Sage to Pink and Terminal, in light or dark, or import your own. Choose an accent color and a message font, and one of four app icons, with a different one for dark mode if you like.

Right-click menus

Right-click a message or a conversation.

Messages get React, Reply, Copy text, Edit, Forward, Pin and Unsend. Conversations get their own menu: mark as read or unread, mute, view profile, archive, delete and more.

Privacy

What Carrier does with your data

No Carrier server

You sign in on Facebook's own page, and your messages go straight between your computer and Facebook.

No tracking

Carrier adds no analytics, and it blocks Facebook's own telemetry by default.

Open source

Every line is on GitHub under the MIT license. Read it, or build Carrier yourself.

Privacy settings

Hide that you're typing, hide names and photos for screen sharing, or hide message previews in notifications.

FAQ

Common questions

Is Carrier really free?

Completely. No price tag, no subscription, no ads, no paid tier. Carrier is open source under the MIT license - made by one developer who missed having Messenger on the desktop.

Do I need a new account?

No - you sign in with your usual account, and all your chats are just there. Anything you send in Carrier shows up on your phone too, and the other way around.

Is this an official Meta app?

No. Meta discontinued their desktop app, so Carrier is an independent, open-source replacement. It shows you the real Messenger - the same one from your browser - in a clean window of its own, with the Facebook clutter tidied away.

Is it safe to use?

Carrier adds zero tracking of its own - it even blocks Facebook's telemetry by default. The Mac version is signed and notarized by Apple, updates are verified before they install, and the complete source code is public for anyone to inspect. Your messages still travel through Messenger itself, so Meta's usual terms apply there. Windows builds are currently unsigned and may show a SmartScreen “Unknown publisher” warning.

Do voice and video calls work?

Yes - it's the full Messenger, so calls, group chats, reactions, photos, and everything else work like you'd expect.

How is the whole app only about 4 MB?

Most chat apps ship an entire hidden web browser inside. Carrier uses the one your computer already has - so it downloads in seconds, starts in a blink, and goes easy on your battery.

What if something stops working?

Facebook changes its markup from time to time, and that can occasionally break a feature. When Automatic Update Checks is enabled, Carrier finds fixes automatically; you choose when to install - and you can always tell us what broke.

Why not just use messenger.com?

Meta shut down messenger.com in April 2026. Messenger now lives inside facebook.com, next to the Feed and Reels. Carrier opens facebook.com/messages in its own window and hides the rest.

Do encrypted chats work?

Yes. End-to-end encrypted chats work like any other chat, and notifications, the unread badge and conversation export cover them too.

Which Mac download do I need?

Open the Apple menu and choose About This Mac. If it says “Chip Apple M…”, download the Apple silicon version. If it says “Processor Intel…”, download the Intel version.

Windows says it protected my PC. What do I do?

Carrier isn't code-signed for Windows yet, so SmartScreen warns about an unknown publisher the first time you open it. Click More info, then Run anyway. Only download Carrier from this site or its GitHub page.

My work computer doesn't let me install apps.

On Windows, use the portable .zip: unzip it anywhere, such as your Downloads folder, and open Carrier from there. It needs no administrator rights. On a Mac, Carrier runs from any folder, not just Applications.

Under the hoodThe more technical answers

How does it work under the hood?

Carrier is a small Rust shell built on Tauri 2 that opens the official Messenger web app in your operating system's own WebView. At page load it injects a focused stylesheet and typed scripts that hide Facebook's surrounding chrome and add the desktop features - notifications, badges, shortcuts, themes. No bundled browser engine, no Electron.

What can the injected code see?

Messenger content is processed only on your machine, only where a feature needs it - turning a new message into a native notification, counting unread chats for the badge, blurring names for screen sharing. Carrier has no servers and phones home to no one; the only network calls it adds are update checks against GitHub Releases. Off-site links open in your browser with Facebook's tracking redirect stripped first.

How do updates work?

When Automatic Update Checks is enabled, Carrier checks GitHub Releases at startup and then every hour, every 4 hours or once a day, as you choose. When a signed update is available, it shows you the release notes. You choose whether to download, install, and restart; Carrier cryptographically verifies the update first. macOS downloads are also Developer ID signed and notarized by Apple. Windows builds are currently unsigned, so SmartScreen may show “Unknown publisher.”

Why is the Linux AppImage bigger than the installers?

The .deb and .rpm packages are about 4 MB because they use your distro's WebKitGTK. The AppImage has to carry its own copy to run anywhere, which is why it weighs around 90 MB. If your package manager is an option, prefer it.

Is there a Homebrew cask?

Yes - brew install --cask kristofferR/tap/carrier, and the same cask token works on macOS and Linux. Already dragged Carrier.app into /Applications? Adopt it with brew install --cask --adopt kristofferR/tap/carrier.

Can I build it myself?

Of course. With Rust, Bun, and the Tauri prerequisites installed: clone the repo, then bun install and bun run build. The injected scripts are typed TypeScript under inject/src/.
