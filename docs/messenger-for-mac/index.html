Chatpane for Messenger / Mac

Updated November 2026

A Messenger desktop app for Mac

Meta's Messenger app for Mac stopped working on December 15, 2025, and messenger.com now redirects to Facebook. Here is what still works on a Mac, and how to get your chats back in a window of their own.

The short answer

There is no official Messenger app for Mac anymore. Meta now sends Mac users to facebook.com/messages in the browser.

If you want a real Mac app instead, Chatpane for Messenger (formerly Carrier) is a free, open-source app that opens that same page in its own window, hides the rest of Facebook, and adds native notifications and a Dock badge. It is unofficial and not made by Meta.

What happened to Messenger for Mac

Meta announced in October 2025 that it would retire its Messenger desktop apps, pulled the Mac app from the Mac App Store, and shut it down on December 15, 2025. Opening it after that pointed you to the Facebook website. In April 2026 Meta also retired messenger.com, so the browser route now goes through facebook.com/messages, inside Facebook. The full timeline has the details and sources.

If you used Messenger without a Facebook account, desktop access ended with messenger.com. Meta says those accounts can keep chatting in the mobile app. Every Mac option below, Chatpane included, needs an account that can sign in at facebook.com.

Your options

Ways to use Messenger on a Mac

Built in

Safari or Chrome

Open facebook.com/messages in a tab. It is Meta's official route and needs nothing installed.

  • Facebook's top bar and links to the Feed stay on screen
  • No Dock icon, and chats close with the browser
Built in

Safari's Add to Dock

On macOS Sonoma and later, choose File, then Add to Dock, while on facebook.com/messages. You get a separate web app window with its own Dock icon.

  • Still the full Facebook page, menus and all
  • Notifications depend on the site and Safari
Recommended

Chatpane for Messenger

A native Mac app, about 4 MB, built on the WebKit engine that already ships with macOS. Only your chats, with the Feed, Reels and Marketplace hidden.

  • Notifications with the sender's photo that you can reply to
  • Unread count on the Dock icon, or a menu bar only mode
  • Signed with a Developer ID and notarized by Apple
Unofficial

Other Mac clients

Messenger Next is another small client, with closed source code. Caprine, the long-running open-source app, was built for messenger.com and has open issues since that site closed.

  • See the Caprine guide for details

Chatpane on the Mac

Chatpane opens the real Messenger, the same one from your browser, so calls, group chats, reactions, photos and your full history are all there and stay in sync with your phone. On a Mac it adds:

  • Only Messenger. Facebook's Feed, Reels, Marketplace and global search are hidden, and the window buttons sit over the chat list instead of a title bar.
  • Notifications like Messages. Native notifications show the sender's face, and you can reply, react, mark as read or mute without opening the app.
  • Dock and menu bar. An unread count on the Dock icon, recent conversations in the Dock menu, and an optional menu bar only mode.
  • Keyboard first. ⌘1 to ⌘9 jump to conversations, ⌘K searches, and an optional ⌘⇧M hotkey shows or hides Chatpane from anywhere.
  • Privacy. Hide that you're typing, hide names and avatars for screen sharing, and block Facebook's analytics requests (on by default).
  • Your look. Light, dark or system appearance, nine built-in themes, an accent color and four app icons.

How to install it

  1. Download the Apple Silicon .dmg, or the Intel .dmg for an older Intel Mac.
  2. Open it and drag Chatpane into your Applications folder. It is notarized by Apple, so macOS only shows its usual first-launch check for downloaded apps.
  3. Launch Chatpane and sign in with your usual Facebook account. Your chats appear right away.

Prefer Homebrew? Run brew install --cask kristofferR/tap/chatpane. Chatpane checks for signed updates and shows the release notes before you install one.

FAQ

Mac questions

Is Chatpane in the Mac App Store?

No. You download it from GitHub or install it with Homebrew. The app is signed with a Developer ID and notarized by Apple, and its complete source code is public.

Does it work on Intel Macs and older macOS versions?

Yes. There are separate downloads for Apple Silicon and Intel, and Chatpane supports macOS 10.15 Catalina and later.

Do voice and video calls work?

Yes. Calls open in their own window, and macOS asks for camera and microphone access the first time, as it does for any app.

Can I sign in with a Messenger-only account?

Not on any desktop app. Chatpane loads facebook.com/messages, and since April 2026 Meta only lets Messenger-only accounts chat in the mobile app.

I used Carrier. Do I need to do anything?

No. Chatpane is the new name for Carrier. The built-in updater renames Carrier.app to Chatpane.app, and your settings and login carry over. Homebrew users get the new chatpane cask with their next upgrade.

Is Chatpane made by Meta?

No. Chatpane for Messenger is an unofficial, independent project, not affiliated with Meta. Your messages still go through Messenger itself, so Meta's terms apply there. Chatpane adds no tracking of its own.
